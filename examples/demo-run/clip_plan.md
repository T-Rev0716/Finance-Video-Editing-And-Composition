- Why this works: Starts and ends on a complete thought; strong opening line.

> Mortgage rates just went back above seven percent. And here's the weird part: the Fed didn't do it. So why is your mortgage getting more expensive? It comes down to one number most people never look at. That number is the ten-year Treasury yield. Think of it as the interest rate the government pays to borrow money for ten years. Banks look at that rate and say, okay, if lending to the government pays this much, a home loan has to pay more. That's the whole trick. So when the ten-year yield goes up, mortgage rates follow it. Not the Fed. The bond market.

> Here's what this means for your wallet. If you already have a fixed-rate mortgage, nothing changes. Your payment is locked. But if you're buying a house right now, the same house costs you hundreds of dollars more every single month than it did a year ago. And that's not because the house got nicer. It's because the price of borrowing money went up. Now why did yields go up in the first place? Three reasons. Oil, inflation, and how much the government is borrowing. When oil gets expensive, almost everything gets more expensive a little later. Gas, shipping, food. That's inflation.
